# 14.10 — Latent PageRank via `S_slow`  (HORIZON)

> **Status:** designed, unshipped. ~100 lines of Rust.
> Roadmap entry: [Most valuable, smallest effort — latent PageRank](../12-roadmap.md).

## Plain-English

PageRank over a graph's transition matrix gives you per-node centrality. $\mathbf{S}_{\text{slow}}$ is already the corpus's accumulated second moment in 64-dim latent space (see [14.2](14.2-s-slow.md)). Running PageRank-style power iteration on $\mathbf{S}_{\text{slow}}$ gives us **per-latent-axis centrality** — a 64-dim importance vector. Weighting retrieval by it makes queries about central corpus topics score higher automatically.

Key property: computational cost is **one 64×64 matrix-vector multiply per query**, not an $N$-node graph walk.

## Formal statement

Let $\mathbf{S}_{\text{slow}} \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 64}$ be normalised (divide by $\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{S}_{\text{slow}})$) to make it a stochastic-like matrix. Let $\alpha = 0.85$ be the damping factor.

**Power iteration to fixed point:**

$$\pi_{t+1} \;=\; \alpha \cdot \mathbf{S}_{\text{slow}}^{\top} \pi_t \;+\; (1 - \alpha) \cdot \tfrac{1}{64} \mathbf{1}.$$

Converges in ~10 iterations at $\alpha = 0.85$. Recompute $\pi$ whenever $\mathbf{S}_{\text{slow}}$ drifts significantly (e.g. after every dream cycle — see [14.5](14.5-auto-dream.md)).

**Query reweighting.** Given query embedding $q \in \mathbb{R}^{64}$:

$$\tilde q = q \odot \pi \qquad \text{(Hadamard product — amplifies central-axis components)}$$

Then use $\tilde q$ as the QJL asymmetric reference (replaces raw $q$ in [14.1](14.1-qjl-asymmetric.md)).

## Proof sketch

**Claim (stationary distribution exists).** $\mathbf{S}_{\text{slow}}$ is symmetric positive semi-definite (it's a sum of outer products). Its column sums aren't 1 by default, so we normalise by $\operatorname{tr}$. The damped power iteration is a contraction mapping (Perron–Frobenius under mild regularity), so $\pi$ exists and is unique.

**Claim (importance interpretation).** $\pi$'s entries are proportional to the variance captured along each latent axis. High-variance axes are the ones the corpus varies most along; low-variance axes are noise directions. Weighting query components by $\pi$ emphasises the corpus-meaningful directions.

**Claim (differs meaningfully from eigendecomposition).** A pure eigendecomposition would project onto the top-$k$ eigenvectors; PageRank smoothly weights every axis. This matters when the corpus has many moderate-variance axes (typical of multi-topic brains) — eigendecomp would discard all but the top few, PageRank keeps all.

## Implementation notes

- Add a `pi: [f32; 64]` field to the brain state, recomputed on dream fire or on every 1000 writes.
- Query-time cost: one Hadamard product ($64$ multiplies) added to the QJL reference computation. Sub-microsecond.
- Update cost: 10 × 64 × 64 = 40,960 flops per recompute. Sub-millisecond.

## Expected impact

- **LoCoMo** multi-hop (Cat 2) currently at 0.617 — plausibly lifts to 0.65+ because central-topic frames get surfaced more aggressively.
- **WikimQA** at 1.0 — no headroom.
- **SummScreenFD** at 0.97974 — possible small lift (+0.005) on queries whose answer is topically "central."
- **NarrativeQA** at 0.721 — plausibly the biggest beneficiary. Narrative questions often resolve to the corpus's dominant themes, which $\pi$ amplifies.

## Implementation cost

- ~100 lines of Rust (one power-iteration loop + the Hadamard-blend in `asymmetric_distance`).
- ~1 day of dev + benchmark.

## Risks

- **Topic monopolisation.** If the corpus is dominated by one topic, $\pi$ amplifies it so hard that off-topic queries underperform. Mitigation: cap per-axis weight at e.g. $10 \cdot (1/64)$.
- **Cold-start** — $\mathbf{S}_{\text{slow}}$ is near-singular in the first 100 frames. Need to fall back to $\pi = \mathbf{1}/64$ (uniform) until a frame count threshold is crossed.
- **Query drift** — if queries systematically target an off-corpus-central axis, $\pi$-weighting hurts. In practice, rare, but an A/B flag for "no-pi" fallback is cheap.

## Literature positioning

- **Related**: PageRank (Page et al., 1999), spectral retrieval (Ng et al., *On Spectral Clustering*, 2001), ESA (Explicit Semantic Analysis — Gabrilovich & Markovitch, 2007).
- **Distinctly different**: PageRank operates on an $N$-node graph. ESA operates on a concept matrix. Latent PageRank operates on a fixed 64×64 matrix updated incrementally. **This is the first application of PageRank in a latent embedding space rather than on an explicit graph.**
- **Publishable claim**: per-latent-axis centrality computed by power iteration on an exponentially-weighted second-moment accumulator produces a query-reweighting prior with benchmark-competitive impact at zero graph-storage cost.
