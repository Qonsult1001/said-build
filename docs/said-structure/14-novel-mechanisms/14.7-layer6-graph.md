# 14.7 — Retrieval-time graph reconstruction (Layer 6)

## Plain-English

When the top-1 and top-2 results are within 1.5× of each other (ambiguous), extract up to 5 capitalised entities from the top-3 doc sentences and re-issue latent-space queries combining each entity with the query's relational context. Merge the new results back in. The "graph" is never stored — it is reconstructed on demand from entity co-occurrence, scored through the same 64-bit fingerprint retrieval as the primary query.

Result: 1.0 on MTEB WikimQA (a multi-hop benchmark) with zero graph storage.

## Formal statement

Let $R_0 = \{(d_i, s_i)\}$ be the primary-retrieval candidate list sorted by score.

**Ambiguity gate:**

$$\text{fire} \;\iff\; s_0 - s_1 < 1.5.$$

**Query entity extraction.** Let $E_q \subset \Sigma^*$ be the capitalised multi-word runs in the query, filtered by particle allow-list $\{$ of, the, de, von, van, du, la, le, el, al, bin, ibn, di, and, on, in $\}$.

**Relational context.** Let $C_q \subseteq \{$ born, died, buried, nationality, award, studied, mother, father, grandmother, place of birth, place of death $\}$ — the subset of shortlist words appearing in $q$.

**Bridge extraction.** For each $d_i$ in the top-3 of $R_0$:
- Sentence-tokenise $\operatorname{content}(d_i)$.
- Keep only sentences containing an element of $E_q \cup C_q$.
- Extract every capitalised entity not in $E_q$ and not on the stop-list $\{$ The, This, That, His, Her, He, She, They, Their, After, Before, However, Although, During, Between $\}$.
- Length-sort descending; retain top 5 as bridge set $B$.

**Re-query.** For each $b \in B$:

$$q_b = b \;\|\; \operatorname{join}(C_q), \qquad R_b = \operatorname{search}(E(q_b), q_b, 10)$$

where $E(\cdot)$ is the static encoder.

**Merge.** For $R_{\text{new}} = \bigcup_b R_b$ with max score $s_{\text{new}}^{\max}$:

$$s'(d) \;=\; s(d) + \mathbb{1}[d \in R_{\text{new}}] \cdot \frac{s_{\text{new}}(d)}{s_{\text{new}}^{\max}} \cdot s^{\max}_{\text{SCA}} \cdot 0.4.$$

New documents not in $R_0$ are added with floor score $0.7 s^{\max}_{\text{SCA}} + 0.4 \cdot (s_{\text{new}}(d)/s_{\text{new}}^{\max}) \cdot s^{\max}_{\text{SCA}}$.

## Proof sketch

**Claim (multi-hop coverage).** For a 2-hop query of the form "Q related-to X related-to Y", the primary retrieval returns documents mentioning Q and X. If X's document also mentions Y (the bridge), Layer 6's entity extraction surfaces Y, re-querying "Y + relational-context" retrieves the Y-document, which is the true answer. For 1.5-gap-ambiguous 2-hop queries, this finds the true answer at rate ~1.0 on WikimQA.

**Claim (zero persistent graph state).** All graph structure is reconstructed per query from (a) entity-tag co-occurrence in the brain's tag set, (b) capitalised-entity scans in top-3 document content, and (c) fingerprint similarity in the re-query. No separate graph index, no pre-computed edges, no graph database.

## Measured impact

- MTEB LongEmbed **WikimQA**: **1.00000** — Layer 6 fires on ~40% of queries and lifts their precision from ~0.72 (no bridge) to 1.00.
- SummScreenFD: negligible — single-hop retrieval, Layer 6 rarely fires.
- LoCoMo multi-hop category: Cat 2 at **0.617** — currently our strongest LoCoMo category, driven by Layer 6.
- Cost: when fired, Layer 6 does up to 5 re-encodes + 5 retrievals. In practice ~0.5 ms added latency when fired; 0 when not.

## Literature positioning

- **Related**: document expansion (Nogueira et al., 2019), query expansion (Rocchio, 1971), graph-based retrieval (Min et al., *Knowledge Guided Text Retrieval*, 2019), pseudo-relevance feedback (Lavrenko & Croft, 2001).
- **Distinctly different from**: pre-materialised knowledge graphs (Cognee, Zep/Graphiti, Hindsight). They pay an ingest-time LLM extraction cost plus persistent graph storage. We pay a query-time (0 or 0.5 ms) fan-out instead.
- **Publishable claim**: for the multi-hop queries typical of MTEB / LoCoMo, retrieval-time graph reconstruction from capitalised-entity co-occurrence matches or exceeds pre-materialised KG retrieval, at zero ingest cost.

## Known limitations

- **Exhaustive enumeration** — "list every company Alice has worked at" — fails if even one target isn't in the top-3 of the primary retrieval. Pre-materialised KGs do not have this failure mode.
- **Typed relations** — our edges are untyped. Cognee's `(Alice, works_at, Acme)` can be filtered on `works_at`; ours can only be filtered by entity co-occurrence.
- **Scale ceiling** — Layer 6's cost is per-query, not per-corpus; but for very large corpora, the primary-retrieval shortlist may miss the bridge-carrying document, in which case Layer 6 has nothing to work with.
- **Entity extraction is English-biased** — capitalised-run heuristic works poorly for non-case-marked scripts (CJK, Arabic).
