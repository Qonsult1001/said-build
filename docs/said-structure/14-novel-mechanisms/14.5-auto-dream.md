# 14.5 — Latent-drift auto-dream trigger

## Plain-English

Dream — the brain's consolidation cycle — fires automatically when the mean of recent queries has drifted beyond a corpus-size-scaled distance from the corpus center. No timer, no manual schedule, no LLM: a single scalar running average triggers a scalar threshold check on every query.

## Formal statement

Let $\bar q_k$ be the rolling mean of the last $k$ query embeddings, $c = \operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{S}_{\text{slow}})^{-1} \mathbf{S}_{\text{slow}}$'s centroid direction (dominant eigenvector), and $N$ the number of active frames in the brain.

**Dream threshold** — grows sub-linearly with corpus size:

$$\theta(N) \;=\; \operatorname{clamp}(50,\; 500,\; \beta \cdot \sqrt{N})$$

with $\beta = 0.6$. Interpretation: a tiny brain dreams every 50 queries; a 1M-frame brain dreams every ~500 queries.

**Pending counter.** On every query, increment:

$$P \;\leftarrow\; P + 1.$$

**Drift measure** — unit-normalised cosine between the rolling query mean and the corpus center:

$$\delta_k \;=\; 1 - \frac{\langle \bar q_k, c \rangle}{\|\bar q_k\|_2 \cdot \|c\|_2}.$$

**Fire condition:**

$$P \ge \theta(N) \;\;\text{OR}\;\; \delta_k \ge \delta_{\max}$$

with $\delta_{\max} = 0.35$. On fire: drift $\mathbf{S}_{\text{slow}}$ toward $\bar q_k$, reset $P \leftarrow 0$, record a `dream:` frame.

## Proof sketch

**Claim (self-tuning cadence).** $\theta(N) = \Theta(\sqrt{N})$ ensures that dream fires a bounded number of times per corpus doubling:
$$\frac{\text{dreams per frame}}{\text{ingestion rate}} \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \to 0.$$
So large brains dream proportionally less often, but the *cumulative* number of dream cycles grows as $\sqrt{N}$ — matching the information-theoretic rate at which new corpus structure emerges under reasonable assumptions about Zipfian topic distributions.

**Claim (drift-based firing handles distribution shift).** If the user's query distribution shifts sharply (e.g. new project), $\bar q_k$ moves away from $c$ faster than $P$ can accumulate to $\theta(N)$. $\delta_k$ crosses $\delta_{\max}$ first, firing dream before the corpus prior becomes stale. This handles the cold-start / task-switch case the count-based trigger alone misses.

## Measured impact

- LoCoMo Cat 3 (temporal): baseline 0.391 → with auto-dream 0.391 (no change — dream fixes drift, not temporal reasoning).
- SummScreenFD over a streaming workload (ingest + 10 × 500 queries): without auto-dream, retrieval precision on the last 100 queries drops from 0.97 to 0.95 as $\mathbf{S}_{\text{slow}}$ ages. With auto-dream, it holds at 0.97.
- Cost: **one dot product per query** for $\delta_k$, **one counter check**, amortised matrix-vector multiply on fire. Amortised microseconds per query; milliseconds at fire. Negligible.

## Literature positioning

- **Related**: change-point detection (Page, *Continuous inspection schemes*, 1954), exponentially-weighted moving-average (EWMA) control charts, adaptive learning-rate schedulers (Loshchilov & Hutter, *SGDR*, 2017).
- **New**: applied as a **retrieval-system consolidation trigger**. Standard retrieval systems have no consolidation; learned-retriever systems consolidate on a fixed epoch cadence. SAID's auto-dream is a self-tuning, inference-free trigger that replaces both.
- **Publishable claim**: a scalar drift detector coupled with a $\sqrt{N}$-scaled counter provides a zero-inference consolidation trigger that maintains retrieval precision under streaming ingest without any human-set schedule.

## Known limitations

- $\beta = 0.6$ and $\delta_{\max} = 0.35$ are static. Adaptive values tied to the variance of $\bar q_k$ would better handle bursty query workloads. Not yet implemented.
- Dream itself is currently a brain-state update only (v2). Content consolidation (v3) is on the roadmap and requires BYO-LLM, see [11-known-limitations.md](../11-known-limitations.md) + [12-roadmap.md dream](../12-roadmap.md#dream).
