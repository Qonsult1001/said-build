# 14.9 — Holographic K-view fingerprint  (HORIZON)

> **Status:** designed, partially wired (hook exists in `ScaEngine`), not exposed, not benchmarked.
> Roadmap entry: [Most differentiating, medium effort — holographic fingerprint](../12-roadmap.md).

## Plain-English

Instead of one 64-bit fingerprint per document, compute $K$ fingerprints each under a different random orthogonal rotation. Retrieval becomes the **median Hamming distance across the $K$ views**, which drives worst-case near-collision probability to near zero. Effective resolution approaches $K \cdot 64$ bits at $K \times$ the storage cost — and retrieval is still XOR+popcount, just $K$ of them.

## Formal statement

Let $R_1, \dots, R_K \in \mathrm{O}(64)$ be independent random orthogonal matrices (fixed once, shipped with the brain).

**Storage.** For each document $d$, store:

$$F_k(d) = \operatorname{sign}(R_k \cdot T_{64}(E(d))) \in 0,1^{64}, \qquad k = 1, \dots, K.$$

Total per-frame: $K \cdot 8$ bytes (e.g. 128 bytes for $K=16$, 256 bytes for $K=32$).

**Retrieval.** Distance between document and query:

$$D_{\text{med}}(d, q) = \operatorname{median}_{k \in 1..K} \operatorname{popcount}\big(F_k(d) \oplus F_k(q)\big).$$

**Why median and not mean.** A mean would be easily biased by a single outlier view where (say) the rotation happened to align a doc-query pair orthogonally. Median is robust to up to $\lfloor K/2 \rfloor$ adversarial views — so an attacker or a pathological corpus structure would need to corrupt ≥ 8 of 16 rotations to affect ranking.

## Proof sketch

**Claim (resolution lift).** Let $p = 1 - \mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{n})$ be the per-dimension sign-preservation probability (from [14.1](14.1-qjl-asymmetric.md)). For $K$ independent rotations:

$$\Pr[\operatorname{median}_k \text{ agrees with true cosine ordering}] \ge 1 - \binom{K}{\lceil K/2 \rceil}(1-p)^{\lceil K/2 \rceil}.$$

At $K = 16$, $p = 0.875$: agreement probability $\ge 1 - 2^{-11}$. This is effectively 1024-bit resolution at $16 \times 8 = 128$ bytes per frame — **3× smaller than a single 4096-bit binary embedding with the same effective resolution**.

**Claim (cost).** Retrieval is $K$ XOR+popcount operations per candidate frame. On x86-64 with SIMD (AVX-512 `vpopcntq`), 8 × 64-bit popcounts fit in one instruction, so $K=16$ at 1M candidates ≈ 2 ms on a single core. Cheaper than running a 768-dim f32 cosine over the same 1M candidates by roughly an order of magnitude.

## Implementation notes

- Rotations are fixed at brain creation, stored in the `MODE` section alongside the brain-mode byte. Brains created with different $K$ values are incompatible — a design-time decision.
- The `K` constant is exposed as a Cargo feature or a runtime `BrainMode` flag; either way, a brain is "flat-1-view" or "holographic-K-view" for its lifetime (same immutability story as portable/enterprise mode).
- QJL rerank (see [14.1](14.1-qjl-asymmetric.md)) still runs on the top-50 shortlist against the *first* view. Median-Hamming is the shortlist mechanism; QJL is the rerank.
- GPU shader (`[gpu_hamming.wgsl](../../../crates/sca-core/src/gpu_hamming.wgsl)`) generalises naturally — each GPU thread handles one view instead of one fingerprint.

## Expected impact

- On adversarial or clustered corpora where plain 1-bit has collisions (multiple near-identical fingerprints for semantically distinct documents), median-Hamming should break the tie.
- Estimated LoCoMo R@10 lift: +0.01 to +0.03. Unclear until benchmarked; this is a precision feature, not a recall feature.
- MTEB LongEmbed: we're already at 1.0 on Needle / Passkey / WikimQA — no lift available. SummScreenFD at 0.97974 has 0.02 of headroom; median-Hamming is the plausible vehicle to close it.

## Implementation cost

- ~200 lines of Rust — `ScaEngine` already has the hook; the work is (a) rotation storage in `MODE`, (b) multi-view encode at ingest, (c) median aggregation at retrieval.
- One-time infrastructure: we'd need to decide whether to upgrade existing brains to K-view (requires re-encoding every frame) or make it a new-brain-only feature. Recommendation: new-brain-only, with a `said convert --to-holographic K=16` migration command.
- Benchmark cost: one MTEB + LoCoMo + chamber run to establish the delta. ~1 day of compute.

## Risks

- **Storage bloat** — 16× fingerprint size is still cheap (128 bytes vs 8 bytes), but at 10M-frame corpora it's 1.28 GB of fingerprints alone. Acceptable for most brains but worth surfacing in stats.
- **Adversarial rotations** — if any of the $R_k$ happens to align with a low-variance axis of the corpus, that view becomes a pure bit-collision trap. Mitigated by using Gaussian-random matrices (not random-signed-permutations) and by the median robustness, but worth A/B testing.
- **Compatibility** — a flat-fingerprint brain can't be queried against a holographic one with the existing API. Needs either cross-compatibility (query-both) or a hard migration.

## Literature positioning

- **Related**: Superbit LSH (Ji et al., *Super-Bit Locality-Sensitive Hashing*, 2012), multi-probe LSH (Lv et al., 2007), bit-parity lifting (Charikar, *Similarity estimation techniques from rounding algorithms*, 2002).
- **Different from**: all of the above cluster-anchor on a single hash or learn an encoder. We ship fixed random rotations (no training), use median aggregation (not union or max), and integrate with an S_slow-based query prior — the combination is novel.
- **Expected claim (horizon, not measured)**: K-view median-Hamming at $K=16$ should approach the collision-resistance of a 1024-bit binary embedding at 12.5% the storage, with retrieval latency still dominated by $K$ XOR+popcount operations. Measurement required on MTEB + LoCoMo + chambers before this is promoted to a publishable result. Do not cite the "1024-bit equivalent" figure externally until the measurement run has completed.
